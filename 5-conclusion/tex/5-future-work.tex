\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:future-work}

Several directions emerge from the limitations identified in the preceding chapters. The most immediate is field validation of the precise-alignment stage: while the SLAM pipeline was evaluated using data collected in operational vineyards, the registration pipeline was tested only indoors on relocated vine specimens, and its behaviour under wind-induced vine motion, variable ambient lighting, surface contamination, and diverse cultivar geometries remains uncharacterised. Field trials should incorporate an independent six-degree-of-freedom pose reference (e.g.\ a motion capture system or precision-surveyed fiducial markers) to establish absolute accuracy bounds for the registration pipeline, which currently reports only internal consistency metrics.

Generalisation across vine specimens and training systems requires evaluation on a larger and more diverse set of vines spanning multiple vineyards, cultivars, and ages. Such testing would determine whether the stereo convergence failures observed at specific distance--angle combinations reflect systematic pipeline vulnerabilities or specimen-specific geometric interactions, and would provide the repeated measurements necessary to quantify run-to-run repeatability.

A related direction is evaluating the LiDAR pipeline in vineyard conditions using the full 360° point cloud rather than the narrow angular aperture used in the indoor trials. In a vineyard row, the accumulated scan captures structure from multiple bays and the opposite row simultaneously. Registering against a correspondingly larger 3DGS model spanning several bays and both adjacent rows would provide substantially more geometric constraint than the single-vine registration evaluated here, potentially improving both convergence reliability and pose precision by exploiting the extended spatial baseline.

On the computational side, two paths to real-time LiDAR alignment are available: reducing the hypothesis count from 35 to 7--10 (exploiting the finding that all hypotheses converge to the same solution), or parallelising the embarrassingly independent GICP runs across multiple cores. Either approach would reduce LiDAR pipeline latency from approximately 46\,s to 10--15\,s without sacrificing reliability. Preprocessing ablation studies that systematically vary filtering parameters while holding the sensing modality constant would disentangle the contributions of sensor physics from algorithmic choices, informing principled parameter selection for new environments.

Finally, end-to-end system integration testing, coupling the SLAM-derived coarse pose with precise-alignment refinement and downstream manipulator path planning, would validate that the millimetre-scale registration accuracy reported here translates to successful physical pruning operations under field conditions.
