\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{chap:Conclusion}

This thesis addressed the localisation requirements of autonomous vineyard pruning robots, where navigation-grade pose estimates are insufficient for millimetre-scale manipulation. A two-stage hierarchical architecture was proposed: a LiDAR-inertial SLAM system provides a coarse global pose, which is subsequently refined through point cloud registration against a pre-built 3D Gaussian Splatting reference model of the target vine.

The SLAM stage (\cref{chap:SLAM}) evaluated three configurations of increasing capability. The final GLIM-GNSS system, which augments LiDAR-inertial odometry with sparse GNSS factor injection, achieved a mean absolute trajectory error of 103\,mm across five vineyard datasets with a mean error rate of 0.022\% per metre---satisfying the low-decimetre accuracy target stated in \cref{sec:research-objectives}. This improves upon standalone LiDAR-inertial odometry, which accumulated unbounded drift on the same vineyard corridors. Evaluation on a held-out dataset confirmed that this performance generalises to unseen routes with near-deterministic run-to-run consistency, demonstrating the reliability required for repeated autonomous operation.

The precise-alignment stage (\cref{chap:precise-alignment}) demonstrated that low-centimetre registration against a 3DGS reference model is achievable with both LiDAR and stereo sensing modalities. The LiDAR pipeline achieved 100\% condition-level convergence with a median inlier RMSE of 33.25\,mm across all nine experimental conditions, confirming reliable registration across all tested approach geometries. The stereo pipeline achieved the lowest registration errors of either modality, with a mean inlier RMSE of 15.51\,mm and performance reaching 12--13\,mm at 80\,cm operating distance. At this range, the stereo pipeline produces pose accuracy approaching what is required for direct manipulator guidance to individual canes. Both modalities satisfy the low-centimetre accuracy target stated in \cref{sec:research-objectives}. The ICP corrections applied ranged from 47 to 165\,mm, consistent with the SLAM system's error magnitude, confirming that the coarse pose reliably places the robot within the registration algorithm's convergence basin.

The two-stage architecture was thus validated in its core design premise: neither stage alone provides what the combined system delivers. SLAM supplies global consistency and sufficient initial accuracy for ICP convergence; ICP refines this estimate to low-centimetre geometric agreement with the reference model. These complementary strengths result in a localisation pipeline spanning global positioning to vine-scale alignment, using only sensors already present on a typical pruning platform. LiDAR offers convergence reliability across all tested conditions, while stereo achieves the lowest absolute error for precision manipulation. Field deployment may further favour LiDAR, where full 360° scans registering against spatially extended multi-bay reference models would provide substantially more geometric constraint than the single-vine aperture evaluated here.
